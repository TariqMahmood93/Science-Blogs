## Why it matters

Language technology increasingly mediates access to information, education and public services. Communities whose languages are poorly supported risk being left behind.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Pashto is spoken by tens of millions of people, mainly in Afghanistan and Pakistan, yet has comparatively little digitised, high-quality text. Models generally perform better in languages that make up a larger share of their training data.
</Evidence>

Common strategies include training a model from scratch on the available text, continuing to train an existing multilingual model on the target language, and transferring knowledge from related or higher-resource languages.

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
It is debated whether small, carefully curated datasets can outperform large but noisy ones, and whether continued training on existing models beats training from scratch for languages with distinct scripts and grammar.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Openly licensed, high-quality text collections and evaluation sets built with native speakers.
- Comparisons of training strategies under the same compute budget.
- Evaluation that covers dialects and everyday use, not only translation.
