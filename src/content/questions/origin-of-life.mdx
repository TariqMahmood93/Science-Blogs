## Why it matters

Understanding how life began would tell us how likely it is to arise elsewhere, and would shed light on the basic machinery shared by all living things.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
All known life uses DNA, RNA and proteins, and shares a common genetic code, which points to a common ancestor. Laboratory experiments have shown that some building blocks of life, such as amino acids, can form from simpler molecules under plausible early-Earth conditions.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
The RNA world hypothesis proposes that RNA, which can both store information and catalyse reactions, came first. Alternatives propose that self-sustaining metabolic reactions came before genetic molecules, or emphasise particular settings such as hydrothermal vents or shallow ponds that dry and refill.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- A self-replicating molecular system built in the laboratory from plausible starting materials.
- Geological evidence narrowing down early-Earth conditions.
- Discovery of life, or its absence, in other environments in the solar system.
