## Why it matters

A result that cannot be reproduced cannot be built on with confidence. In a fast-moving field, unreproducible results can steer many groups in the wrong direction.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Many machine learning venues now encourage or require sharing code and data, and reproducibility checklists have become common. Results can still vary with random seeds, hardware, software versions and small implementation details.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
One view is that openness is improving and tools make reruns easier. Another is that the largest results are now produced by systems too expensive to retrain, and sometimes not released at all, so they cannot be checked in the usual way.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Reporting results across several random seeds with variance, as standard.
- Independent replication studies that are published and valued.
- Ways to check claims about large models without retraining them, such as shared evaluation access.
