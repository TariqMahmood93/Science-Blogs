## Why it matters

How we answer shapes how results are credited, how they are reviewed, and how far we can rely on automated science.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Philosophers of science have long distinguished the process of generating a new idea from the process of justifying it. In current practice, machine-generated candidates are typically checked by people, experiments or formal verification before they are accepted.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
Some argue a discovery requires understanding why a result is true, which current systems may lack. Others argue that a novel, verified and useful result is a discovery regardless of who or what produced it, and that understanding can come afterwards.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Clear reporting of which steps a machine performed and which people performed.
- Cases where a machine-generated result was confirmed independently and changed a field's thinking.
- Norms for credit and authorship that journals and funders adopt.
