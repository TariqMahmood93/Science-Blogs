## Why it matters

Copyright, privacy law and research integrity all depend on knowing what data a model learned from. Without reliable methods, claims in either direction are hard to settle.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Membership inference attacks try to decide whether a specific example was in a model's training data, often by checking whether the model is unusually confident on it. They are well studied for smaller models and are also a recognised privacy risk.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
For very large models trained on huge corpora, it is debated how reliable these tests are. A model may be confident on text it never saw because similar text was common, which produces false positives.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Tests validated on models whose training data is fully known.
- Watermarking or fingerprinting methods that let data owners check use in advance.
- Standards for documenting training data, so detection is not the only option.
