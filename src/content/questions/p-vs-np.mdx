## Why it matters

Much of modern cryptography relies on some problems being hard to solve even though answers are easy to check. If P equalled NP in a practical way, many of these protections would fail, and many hard optimisation problems would become easy.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
P is the class of problems that can be solved in polynomial time; NP is the class whose proposed solutions can be verified in polynomial time. Every problem in P is in NP. The question is whether the reverse holds. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems.
</Evidence>

Many important problems, such as Boolean satisfiability, are NP-complete: a fast algorithm for any one of them would give fast algorithms for all problems in NP.

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
Most complexity theorists expect that P does not equal NP. Some known proof techniques have been shown to be unable to settle the question, which suggests genuinely new ideas are needed.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- New lower-bound techniques that avoid the known barriers.
- Proofs of separation for restricted models of computation that point towards the general case.
