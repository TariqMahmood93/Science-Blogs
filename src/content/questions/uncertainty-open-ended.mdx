## Why it matters

Readers need to know which parts of a generated answer to double-check. A system that flags its own weak spots would be far safer than one that sounds equally confident about everything.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
For prediction tasks with fixed answers, calibration can be measured directly: predictions made with 80% confidence should be right about 80% of the time. Many methods exist to measure and improve calibration in this setting.
</Evidence>

For open-ended text, approaches include sampling several answers and checking whether they agree in meaning, asking the model to state its confidence, and checking individual claims against sources.

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
It is unclear whether a model's stated confidence reflects anything about its internal state, or is simply more generated text. Agreement between samples is also imperfect: a model can be consistently wrong.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Uncertainty scores that predict which individual claims in a long answer are wrong.
- Evaluations on questions where the true answer is unknown to the model's training data.
- Interfaces that communicate uncertainty in ways readers actually use.
