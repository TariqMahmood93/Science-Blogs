## Why it matters

Dark matter appears to shape how galaxies form and how the universe's large-scale structure developed. Identifying it would connect cosmology with particle physics.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Several independent observations point to unseen mass: the rotation speeds of stars in galaxies, the way galaxy clusters bend light from objects behind them, and patterns in the cosmic microwave background. Dark matter does not appear to emit or absorb light.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
Leading candidates include new kinds of particles, such as weakly interacting massive particles and axions, though direct detection experiments have not confirmed any of them. A minority of researchers instead propose modifying our theory of gravity, but these approaches struggle to explain all the observations together.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Direct detection of a dark matter particle in a laboratory experiment.
- Observations that clearly rule out whole classes of candidates.
- Evidence that consistently favours either new particles or modified gravity across different scales.
