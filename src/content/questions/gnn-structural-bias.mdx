## Why it matters

Graph models are used for recommendations, fraud detection and social network analysis. If the structure of the network disadvantages some groups, a model that leans on that structure can pass the disadvantage on.

## What we know

<Evidence kind="established">
Graph neural networks compute a representation for each node by combining information from its neighbours. Many real networks show homophily: similar people tend to connect with each other. As a result, a node's neighbourhood can encode sensitive attributes even when those attributes are removed from the data.
</Evidence>

## Where the debate is

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
Researchers disagree on where to intervene: by rewiring or reweighting edges, by constraining what the model learns, or by adjusting predictions afterwards. Each approach can trade off accuracy against fairness differently, and results vary across datasets.
</Evidence>

## What would count as progress

- Shared benchmarks with realistic graphs and clearly defined sensitive attributes.
- Analysis separating bias that comes from features, from structure, and from missing information.
- Methods whose fairness holds when the graph changes over time.
