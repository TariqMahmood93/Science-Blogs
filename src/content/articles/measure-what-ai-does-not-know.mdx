When a weather forecast says 70% chance of rain, we can check it: on days given that forecast, it should rain about seven times in ten. That property is called **calibration**, and it is one of the main ways researchers measure whether a model knows what it does not know.

## Two kinds of not knowing

<Evidence kind="established">
Researchers usually separate uncertainty that comes from noise in the world, which more data cannot remove, from uncertainty that comes from the model's limited knowledge, which more data can reduce<Cite id="uncertainty-types" />.
</Evidence>

## Beyond calibration

- Abstention: letting the model decline to answer, and measuring the accuracy of what it does answer.
- Out-of-distribution detection: noticing when an input is unlike anything seen in training.
