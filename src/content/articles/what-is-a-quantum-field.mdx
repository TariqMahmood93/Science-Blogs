This is an **explainer** of a widely accepted framework, not a report of a new result. Where the explanation involves judgement about pictures and metaphors, I say so.

## From particles to fields

The common picture is wrong but useful: an electron is a tiny ball flying through space. The picture that reproduces experiments is different. The electron field exists everywhere, at all times, and an electron is a localised excitation of that field — a ripple with a definite energy and momentum<Cite id="qft-textbook" />. The same holds for every known matter particle and every force carrier: each has its own field.

<Evidence kind="established">
Quantum field theory, built on this picture, produces predictions that match experiment to twelve significant figures in the best-tested case, the magnetic moment of the electron.
</Evidence>

## Why the field, not the particle, is fundamental

Two features of the theory only make sense if the field, not the particle, is the basic object.

1. **Particle number is not conserved.** In a quantum field, particles can be created and destroyed by energy exchange — pair production, Higgs decays, radioactive emission. A theory of fixed little balls cannot describe this; a theory of excitations in a field does so naturally.
2. **Vacuum is not empty.** The lowest-energy state of each field still has quantum fluctuations. These are not a philosophical quibble: they shift atomic energy levels (the Lamb shift) and change the magnetic moment of the electron, both measured to high precision<Cite id="g-2-measurement" />.

<Evidence kind="interpretation">
Textbooks sometimes say the vacuum "boils with virtual particles". That phrase is a metaphor for a mathematical expansion. The measurable effects are real; the little particles popping in and out are a visualisation of the maths, not a direct observation.
</Evidence>

## The Higgs case

The 2012 discovery of the Higgs boson at the Large Hadron Collider was the discovery of an excitation of a field that had been predicted since the 1960s<Cite id="higgs-discovery" />. The experiment did not confirm "a particle existed". It confirmed that the Higgs field exists, by producing a ripple in it with the predicted properties.

<Evidence kind="established">
Every known non-gravitational force and matter particle is now described within quantum field theory, and this framework is the backbone of the Standard Model.
</Evidence>

## Where the picture ends

Gravity, so far, resists the same treatment. Treating general relativity as a quantum field theory of a graviton field produces infinities that cannot be removed by the standard techniques. Whether this is a solvable technical problem or a sign that one of the two frameworks must change is an open research question.

</Evidence>
